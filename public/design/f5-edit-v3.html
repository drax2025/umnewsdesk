Article Body 3 interlinks placed

Aberdeen City Council's latest housing needs assessment has revealed that 1,840 households are on the statutory homeless register, the highest figure recorded since 2013, amid warnings from housing charities that the city's temporary accommodation stock has reached capacity.

The data, disclosed in response to a Freedom of Information request filed by The Chronicle in October, shows a 23% rise in applications since the same period last year. Families with dependent children now account for 41% of all applicants — a proportion housing officers say reflects a broader collapse in the private rented sector across the city's western suburbs.

Aberdeen City Council confirmed that the council's allocation committee had met three times in November to discuss emergency provision options. Sources familiar with the meetings said councillors were presented with a report recommending the temporary conversion of four sheltered housing blocks in Torry, Kincorth, and Northfield into emergency family accommodation.

"The scale of need here is something we have not seen since the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis," said Rachel Gilmour, chief executive of Aberdeen-based housing charity Shelter North East Scotland. "The private rented market has essentially failed a large proportion of families on low incomes. Council supply cannot absorb this shock at the current rate."

The council's housing directorate declined to comment on specific allocation decisions ahead of the December committee meeting. A spokesperson said the authority was "working urgently and in line with its legal obligations" to ensure all households assessed as being at risk received a formal offer before 20 December.

The ONS regional housing affordability index, released last month, ranked Aberdeen 12th most unaffordable for renters among 30 Scottish council areas, a rise of four places from the previous year. Average monthly private rents in Aberdeen rose 11.4% year-on-year to £1,147 in the third quarter, according to the Scottish Government's lettings register.

Shelter North East Scotland has called on the Scottish Government to invoke emergency housing powers available under the Housing (Scotland) Act 2014. The organisation estimates that approximately 340 of the 1,840 households currently on the register are at acute risk of sleeping rough by mid-December if emergency measures are not implemented within 14 days.

The council has so far confirmed 214 emergency placements in B&B or short-term lets since October, at a cost the directorate estimates at £3.2m over the winter period. Aberdeen's housing emergency declarations and council responses since 2020 are archived on The Chronicle's housing investigations page.

A committee report dated 14 November, obtained by The Chronicle, indicates that officers had recommended a formal housing emergency declaration — a step the council leader has not yet confirmed publicly.
